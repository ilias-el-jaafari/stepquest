# OpenStreetMap and Overpass etiquette

StepQuest runs no server. Every live map comes from public Overpass instances run by volunteers, so the client must stay polite. The rules below are implemented in `src/osm/overpass.ts`, `src/osm/download.ts`, `src/osm/coarse.ts` and `src/world/streamer.ts`; keep them intact. The reasons for the numbers are in `.kiro/specs/map-rendering/design.md` ("Root cause" and "Map loading v2"); change a limit only together with that section.

- **Cache first.** Look in IndexedDB (7-day TTL, `CACHE_TTL_MS`) and in the area index for a cached area that already covers the request before any network call. Cache keys include the codec version and grow the box outwards to a 0.001° grid (about 100 m) so nearby requests share an entry and an entry always covers its key; the grown box is what is downloaded and what the size cap measures. Expired, evicted and older-version entries are deleted locally after a save; this never sends a request. A damaged entry counts as a miss.
- **Two requests at most per tab, spaced.** `MAX_IN_FLIGHT` (2) downloads at once, starts at least `MIN_REQUEST_GAP_MS` (1.5 s) apart, and at most 12 starts in any 60 s (`MAX_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE`). A third is refused (`busy`), not queued; an explicit "load this area" download runs alone. Why not 1 and 10 s as before: Overpass limits each IP by slots (typically two on the main servers; `/api/status` shows it) and answers 429 when they are taken, so two is what a polite client may hold; one request with a 10 s gap made a 12-cell view take 30 s at best (design.md, Root cause). This doubles the worst-case request rate (6 to 12 a minute) and raises the cells per request from 4 to 6, so the real bound is demand: a cold quest start needs about 6 requests, a 90-minute walk about 10 more, and a second visit nothing for 7 days (cache). Revisit if the mirrors start answering 429 to normal use.
- **Size cap.** Refuse areas above `MAX_AREA_KM2` (36 km²). Ask only for the area a walk needs (`areaForWalk`), and `out geom` for ways only: highways, buildings, parks and green landuse, water, waterways. Relations are not requested (bigger answers); the parser reads multipolygon relations if an extract has them, so adding them to the query is an owner decision.
- **Mirrors, hedged.** The public mirrors in `OVERPASS_ENDPOINTS` are asked in order, the last good one first. The next mirror is asked only when the one before failed or has sent no answer header for `HEDGE_MS` (6 s); the first good header wins and the others are aborted before they send a body (never three bodies over one phone link, never three times the load on volunteers). Limits: header 25 s (`HEADER_TIMEOUT_MS`), stall 12 s (`STALL_TIMEOUT_MS`), one call 60 s (`TOTAL_TIMEOUT_MS`), the client deadline 75 s (`REQUEST_DEADLINE_MS`) that frees the slot and ends the worker. Answer 429 and 504 as `rateLimited`. An answer without an `elements` array is `badResponse` and a timeout remark is `busy`; neither is cached. Do not add mirrors that forbid this use.
- **Back-off with jitter.** A failed unit (a cell or a coarse region) waits 4 s ±25 %, doubling to 2 min; a 429 or 504 pauses every start for at least 20 s ±25 %. Jitter so many phones do not retry together. No unit is given up for good while the view needs it, and a failing unit never delays another. Why shorter than the old 30 s to 5 min: on a mobile link most failures are transient and the old wait cost more than the retry; the pause after a 429 is what protects the server.
- **Streaming (requirements 19 and 20).** Two layers through the same `OverpassClient`. Coarse: one request per 0.05° region (at most 24 km²; roads from motorway to living street and pedestrian, big green and blue areas, rivers, rail; no buildings, barriers, paths, lawns or points of interest; about 0.075 MB of raw JSON per km², 11 times less than detail), cached as one entry per region (`v<codec>:coarse:i,j`, 7 days, at most `MAX_COARSE_ENTRIES` 16). Detail: fixed 0.01° cells, 1 to 6 per request (`BATCH_MIN`, `BATCH_MAX`, at most 6 km²), starting at 2 and following what the link finishes; asked only at zooms above `FAR_ZOOM`, where buildings are drawn. Cache first per cell and region (`v<codec>:cell:i,j`, 7 days, at most `MAX_CELL_ENTRIES` 300), read at once and without waiting for the view to rest; a cache read stuck for 2 s is a miss and a write stuck for 5 s is not saved. Nothing is sent while the view moves (`SETTLE_MS`, 0.5 s), only for what the view, its furthest-out version (at most 6 coarse regions) and, with a route, the next 1.2 km of it (at most 6 cells, only while no other download runs) need, nearest first, and never while offline. The look-ahead is new: a walker crosses a cell border about every 12 minutes, so without it the next cell starts loading when it is already on screen; it is bounded, cached for a week and keeps one slot free for the view. While a route or quest exists the frame follows the view (requirement 20.17); that adds no request of its own, it only lets the rules above apply to what is on screen. Do not add any other prefetching or retries outside the back-off above.
- **Identification.** Browsers send their own Referer and User-Agent. Node scripts must send a descriptive User-Agent with a contact address.
- **Tests and CI never touch the network.** They use the bundled fixture or a mocked `fetch`.
- **Attribution (ODbL).** The text "© OpenStreetMap contributors" (`OSM_ATTRIBUTION`; `map.attribution` in both languages, Spanish "© colaboradores de OpenStreetMap"), linked to `https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright`, is always visible on the map, in the bundled demo and in live data, in the bottom-left corner (requirement 18). Never hide, shrink to unreadable size or move it behind a menu. The bundled extract `src/data/benicalap.osm.json` is ODbL data: keep it credited, and rebuild it only with `npm run fixture`.
